# PDBSplat

**TypeScript + React + three.js + React Three Fiber + Vite + WebGPU**

PDBSplat renders proteins from the [Protein Data Bank](https://www.rcsb.org) as Gaussian splats, in the browser. Each atom becomes a soft blob of colour at its place in the structure, so a protein reads as a cloud of matter rather than as balls and sticks. Each splat is an ellipsoid, a Gaussian as much as a solid, drawn by a shader of PDBSplat's own: every pixel crosses its own ray with the ellipsoid itself, so the same thing can be drawn as soft density or as a solid surface.

**Live demo: [snovakow.com/pdbsplat](https://snovakow.com/pdbsplat/)**

## What it shows

The page opens on 1IGT, an intact mouse antibody of almost 13,000 atoms: four protein chains in a Y, with two sugar chains in its stem. The panel lists five more examples, from small to large: 1CRN, crambin, a small plant protein of 327 atoms; 1BNA, a short stretch of DNA; 1EJG, crambin again with its hydrogens, alternate conformations and the directions each atom moves in; 1D3Z, ubiquitin as solved by NMR, in several models; and 4HHB, human haemoglobin, four protein chains, each holding a heme with its iron. Any other entry loads by its four-character PDB ID. You can find more by searching [rcsb.org](https://www.rcsb.org). An entry is downloaded compressed and read in the background as it arrives, and **Status** in the panel follows the download. A small one, every example among them, appears whole once it's read. A large one appears a few chains at a time as its file is read, the structure gliding to keep what has arrived centred and the view easing out, never in, to keep it framed as more of it comes, until the file ends and the view settles on the whole entry; even the largest, with more than a gigabyte of text and eleven million atoms, draw and answer the pointer all the while, with no pause longer than a moment. While one arrives the page draws ten frames a second, which leaves the processor to reading the file, and every frame while you drag, zoom or pinch the view and for a moment after. The surface and the density field are made once the whole entry has arrived. How deeply each atom is buried is worked out after the whole file is read, in the background: the atoms darken as that lands, at once on the examples and a few minutes later on the largest entries, where the shading sweeps across the structure over a moment.

Each atom is one round splat, sized by its element's van der Waals radius. The page opens on the first of the panel's presets, space-filling: the splats shaded as solid, glossy spheres, each chain in its own colour, the buried atoms and the far side of the structure in shadow, ambient occlusion darkening the contacts between atoms, and a glow around the brightest highlights; the panel turns each of these down or off, or sets another look at once. Coloured by element instead, each atom takes its element's CPK colour: carbon grey, nitrogen blue, oxygen red, sulphur yellow, phosphorus and iron orange. Where an entry holds several models, as NMR structures do, PDBSplat starts on the first, and where residues have alternate conformations, on the first of each; **Models** and **Alternates** in the panel show the rest. As it loads, each entry is centred, turned about its upright axis to show its broadest side, and framed to fit the window, on the atoms it starts with, so showing more of them never moves it. A large entry is turned by the first of its chains to arrive, and keeps that turn as the rest come in.

## Controls

- **Drag** to orbit the structure, **scroll** or pinch to zoom, and **right-drag** or drag with two fingers to pan. The structure turns slowly on its own while **Spin**, in the Scene folder, is on, as it is when the page opens.
- **Point** at an atom, in any style, and a label beside the pointer names it: its atom name and element, and its alternate conformation if it has one; its residue, with any insertion code, its chain, and its model in an entry with several; its B-factor, and its occupancy when the atom is only partly there. The label hides while you drag.
- **Click** or tap an atom to pick it, and click empty space to let it go. Even a faint atom can be picked, but not one that Focus has faded far out.
- The **panel** at the top right. Its folders hide the controls the chosen style doesn't use, and any that the other settings or the loaded entry leave nothing to change, so that no control shows that would draw the same picture wherever you set it. Atoms and Scene start folded: click a folder's name to open or close it.
  - **Preset**, at the top, sets every control of the look at once, and leaves **Hydrogens**, **Waters**, **Models**, **Alternates** and **Spin** as they are. Once a control that shows has moved from the preset's setting, the list reads custom, which it lists only then, and choosing the preset again puts its look back. It holds eleven looks, each named for what it draws, and chosen by eye on the antibody to show off one thing the renderer does:
    - **space-filling**, the look the page opens on: every atom a solid, glossy sphere of its van der Waals radius
    - **B-factor**, solid spheres coloured by their B-factors and swollen by them, so that the mobile loops bulge red around a still, blue core
    - **residue ellipsoids**, one solid, glossy ellipsoid per residue, the merged shape of its atoms
    - **transparent shell**, see-through spheres two layers deep: the nearest surface over the back shell
    - **backbone focus**, the backbone as solid beads coloured along each chain, inside the side chains as faint dark glass, which dims the beads it lies in front of
    - **ligand focus**, the ligands and the residues that hold them solid and bright, inside everything else as faint dark glass, which dims them where it lies in front of them. In an entry without ligands every atom stays solid, shaded by ambient occlusion
    - **molecular surface**, the surface in matte colour, shaded in its grooves
    - **outlined surface**, the surface in flat colour with its folds inked in black, as in an illustration
    - **Gaussian splats**, every atom a soft Gaussian, summed into a cloud
    - **backbone glow**, the backbone alone, as small Gaussians that add their light together, coloured along each chain
    - **density field**, the atoms' density as a cloud, coloured along each chain
  - **Example** loads one of the six examples, and **PDB ID** and **Load** fetch any other entry.
  - **Look:**
    - **Style** draws soft Gaussian splats, or hard-edged ones shaded as spheres, which at full size make a space-filling model, or a surface: a smooth molecular surface meshed by marching cubes, on which the atoms' colours meet along clean boundaries, or a field: the atoms' density drawn as a cloud, denser where atoms crowd and fading away at the structure's edge, coloured by the atom nearest each point.
    - **Resolution**, for the surface and the field, sets how finely the surface is meshed or the field is gridded, in grid points per ångström: from 0.5 to 4, where higher shows more detail, and takes longer. Large entries are meshed more coarsely where needed, to keep within about ten million triangles, and gridded more coarsely to keep the field within a few million points.
    - **Size** scales every atom, and **Opacity** sets how much they let through, in every style; in the field it sets how thick the cloud is. At full Opacity, where it starts, the spheres are solid: each hides what's behind it, and spheres that meet cut into one another along curved creases, as in a space-filling model. Below it they turn see-through together, as one layer: each pixel shows the nearest sphere's surface, with its creases, over whatever lies behind the structure, and no sphere shows through another.
    - **Back shell**, for the spheres and the surface below full Opacity, draws the far side of the structure under its near side, so that it reads as hollow glass, two layers thick where it was one: the far creases of the spheres show through the near ones, and the surface's inner wall shows through its outer. Each side is one layer. Where a line of sight crosses the structure more than once, as through a groove or a cavity, the spheres show their farthest surface behind their nearest, and the surface its nearest inner wall behind its nearest outer one, so what lies between the two is hidden. It starts off, and shows only below full Opacity. At full Opacity the surface and the solid spheres hide their far side, and the atoms that Focus fades, or that Alternates draws at their occupancy, stay one layer over the solid ones: a far side isn't drawn in among solid spheres.
    - **Hardness**, for the soft splats, firms their edges, from a soft Gaussian falloff to a flat disc.
    - **Shading**, **Highlight**, **Shininess** and **Outline**, for the spheres and the surface, which are lit alike, from the viewer: they set how far a surface darkens where it turns away, how bright and how tight its highlight is, and the width of an inked rim where it turns edge on, which outlines each sphere and the surface's folds. **Shininess** shows only while **Highlight** is above 0, as there's otherwise no highlight to tighten.
    - **Blend**, for the soft splats, sets how they're laid over one another. In normal they're summed: a soft splat has no surface to layer, so each adds its colour by how near and how opaque it is, and the structure reads like a cloud. In glow, each adds its light to what's behind it, so dense parts glow like an X-ray, with **Opacity** setting how brightly. Neither depends on the order the splats are drawn in, so nothing flips as the view turns. The spheres take no Blend: they're always layered by the depth of their surfaces, as above. Summed or glowing, a sphere would be a disc, which a soft splat already is at full **Hardness**.
    - **Detail**, for the soft splats and the spheres, draws the atoms themselves, or one blob per residue or per chain, each the merged shape of the atoms it stands for, in their mixed colour: a residue spread along its side chain becomes an ellipsoid along it. In the spheres style the blobs are solid ellipsoids, layered as the spheres are. On auto it draws whichever is still a few pixels across, so the atoms give way to residues as you zoom out and come back as you zoom in. Pointing at a blob still names the atom under the pointer.
  - **Colour:**
    - **Colour by** colours each atom by one of five things, in every style:
      - its **element**, as described above
      - its **chain**, which gives every chain its own hue
      - its **B-factor**, which says how far the atom moves about its place: blue for the entry's stillest atoms, through white, to red for its most mobile, so a protein's ordered core reads blue and its loose loops red
      - a **rainbow** along each chain, from blue at its start to red at its end, while ligands and waters keep their element colours
      - its secondary **structure**, in Jmol's colours: helices pink, strands yellow and turns blue, DNA purple and RNA red outside those, and the rest of a chain white, while ligands and waters keep their element colours
      - The B-factor's spread, a chain's length along the rainbow and the secondary structure are the whole entry's, so while a large entry arrives the B-factor colouring is white, the rainbow keeps every atom in its element's colour, and the structure colouring shows what's known so far, each taking its colours once the entry has all arrived.
    - **Burial** darkens each atom by how deeply the atoms around it bury it, in every style, so a protein's core falls into shadow while its surface stays bright.
  - **Atoms:**
    - **Hydrogens** and **Waters** show or hide those atoms. Hydrogens start shown and waters hidden. Each of the four controls that choose which atoms are shown, these two and the next two, shows only for an entry that has what it chooses among.
    - **Models**, in every style, shows an entry's first model, or all its models at once, or plays them in turn as an animation. An NMR entry has several, and most other entries one. With all of them shown, the soft splats draw each model fainter, so that where the models disagree reads as a cloud, and the spheres draw every model solid, while the surface meshes them together into one and the field sums them into one cloud.
    - **Alternates**, in every style, shows only the first conformation of each residue the structure records in more than one position, or every one of them. The soft splats and the spheres draw each alternate atom as faint as its occupancy, the share of the time it spends there, the spheres in their see-through layer, where an atom there half the time keeps half its light and hides most of what's behind it, while the surface and the field take them all together.
    - **Thermal**, for the soft splats and the spheres, blurs or swells each atom by how far its B-factor says it moves, so mobile loops spread out while the core stays crisp.
    - **Ellipsoids**, for the soft splats and the spheres, draws each atom as its thermal ellipsoid in place of its van der Waals size: the region it spends half its time in. Where the entry records how far each atom moves in each direction, as very high-resolution crystal structures do, the ellipsoid stretches along the way the atom moves most; elsewhere it's a round one, sized by the B-factor. In the spheres style the ellipsoids are solid, and cut into one another as the spheres do. **Size** scales the ellipsoids, and **Thermal** steps aside while they're shown.
    - **Focus**, for the soft splats and the spheres, keeps every atom in focus, or only the ligands and the residues that hold them, or only the backbone: a protein's main chain, or a nucleic acid's sugars and phosphates, or only the picked atom's residue or its whole chain. **Fade** sets how faintly the atoms out of focus are drawn, and at 0 hides them. It shows for any Focus but all, though not for the ligands in an entry that has none, where every atom stays in focus. The ligands' binding sites are found once the whole entry has arrived, so while a large one arrives every atom stays in focus. The spheres draw the faded atoms as one see-through layer over the solid ones in focus: the nearest faded surface under each pixel, with the atoms in focus showing through it. A faded sphere hides more of what's behind it than it keeps of its own light, as much as it looks to be there: at a tenth of its light it looks a third as bright, and hides six tenths. So an atom in focus is dimmed by the faded atoms in front of it, and reads as deep in the structure as it is, rather than shining through as if it lay in front of them. Low on the slider the faded atoms are faint dark glass, and by halfway they're dim and all but solid.
  - **Scene:**
    - **Spin**, on as the page opens, turns the structure slowly about its y axis, upright as the page frames it, in every style: once every 30 seconds, or more slowly where the page draws fewer than ten frames a second. A drag holds the turn while it moves the view, and the turn eases back in from wherever the drag leaves it. It starts off for a visitor whose system asks for reduced motion.
    - **Depth cue** darkens the far side of the structure, in every style, so its depth reads at a glance. It stays tied to the structure as you orbit, zoom or pan.
    - **Clip** cuts away the near part of the structure, in every style, to show its inside: halfway along, the front half is gone. The cut faces you whichever way you orbit. The spheres, the surface and the field show where they're cut, each sphere with a flat face in its atom's colour, and where cut faces overlap each point shows the atom it lies deepest inside, while the soft splats fade out as they come through the cut.
    - **Bloom** spreads a soft glow around the bright parts of the scene, in every style. While it's on, **Bloom radius** and **Bloom threshold** below it set how far the glow spreads and how bright a part must be to give off any.
    - **AO**, for the spheres and the surface, adds ambient occlusion: it shades the contacts between atoms and the grooves of the surface, where light from all around is partly blocked. It reads the depth of what's in view, so it works, and shows, only while everything drawn is opaque: the spheres while none of them is see-through, or the surface at full Opacity. While it's on, **AO radius** below it sets how far around each point it looks for what shades it, in ångströms.
  - **Info:** **Use WebGL 2** switches to the WebGL 2 fallback described below, and **Renderer** says which one is drawing. **Entry**, **Atoms shown** and **Picked** report the entry's ID and title, how many atoms are drawn and the picked atom. **Status** reports, while an entry loads, how much of its compressed file has arrived of the whole and how many atoms have been read, then whether its burial is still being worked out, or why a load failed.
- Every slider but **Size**, **Resolution** and **Thermal** takes effect at once, as it doesn't reshape the atoms or sample the field again, and so do **Colour by**, **Focus** and a pick, which recolour the atoms in place. The splats are kept on the GPU in stores of a fixed size, one for each level of detail: each chain is written into its level's store as it first comes into view, and one out of view isn't drawn. A level whose splats don't all fit its store isn't drawn at all: auto chooses among the rest, and a fixed **Detail** draws the next coarser level that fits, so the largest entries show their residues or their chains rather than every atom. **Size**, **Thermal** and **Ellipsoids** reshape the atoms, and the blobs with them; **Hydrogens**, **Waters**, **Models** and **Alternates** choose the atoms again, whose residues and chains are merged again in the background, what's drawn standing until they are; and **Detail** chooses the levels. Each refills the stores, without making them anew, and on auto a level is filled only once the view first draws it. **Opacity** rebuilds the spheres only as it reaches full or leaves it, and **Focus** and **Fade** only as they give the spheres a see-through layer or take it away. **Back shell** rebuilds the spheres and leaves the surface's mesh alone.
- The counter at the top left shows the frame rate.

PDBSplat renders with WebGPU where the browser offers it. Elsewhere, or if WebGPU fails once it's running, it uses three.js's WebGL 2 fallback, which draws the same picture. **Use WebGL 2** in the panel forces the fallback, and adding `?webgl` to the page's address starts with it on.

## Running it locally

You'll need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) 22.12 or later, which Vite 8 and drei's camera controls require. Then, from the repository's folder:

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

and open the address it prints.

To build it, run `npm run build`. It makes a static site in `dist/` that works from any folder on any web server, and `npm run preview` serves it locally. `npm test` runs the tests, on Node's own test runner, and needs Node.js 22.18 or later, which runs TypeScript without a flag.

## Status

PDBSplat loads and draws any entry in the Protein Data Bank in the four styles above, names the atom under the pointer, and colours, fades, blurs, cuts, shades and animates the structure with the controls above, on WebGPU or on WebGL 2. Nothing it draws is sorted, so nothing flips or flickers as the view turns: the spheres are layered by their surfaces' depths, pixel by pixel, and everything else is summed. The spheres and the surface are lit by one rule, every style is shaded by depth and cut by one rule each, and the panel shows a control only while it has something to change. The next steps: see-through spheres more than two layers deep, and a fade between the levels of detail as the view zooms.

## Workflow

- **Planned in phases.** The project was set up by following a written plan. Each phase was one focused thread, and the phases that changed code ended with lint and a type-checked production build passing.
- **Reviewed before it's committed.** Every batch of work goes through `/commit`, a [Claude Code skill](.claude/skills/commit/SKILL.md) that serves as the final code review. It's adapted from a shared [baseline](https://github.com/snovakow/Claude-Skills/tree/main/skills/commit), and it runs in a fresh thread, so the review starts without the author's assumptions. It reads every changed line for bugs, React Three Fiber and three.js pitfalls, type and lint escapes, new dependencies, leftovers and anything that shouldn't be public. Then it runs the checks, the tests among them, splits the work into focused commits with descriptive messages, and keeps this README accurate.
- **Tested where it's pure.** The parts with no GPU or React in them, such as the mmCIF reader, the surface mesher, the colourings and the rules that decide what's shown, have tests that build their own inputs and pin what each guarantees, and each review runs them with the other checks.
- **Versioned checkpoints.** Versions start at 0.0.0 and count up by what each release holds: the middle number for a feature, the last for anything less, and the first for a milestone. Each reviewed batch ends with a version commit, which marks where the next review starts and adds the release's notes to the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md).
- **Current dependencies.** Packages and tools track their latest releases, and each review reports anything that has fallen behind.
- **Branches and deploys.** Work lands on `development` and reaches `main` by merge once it has settled. The live site is a static build of `main`.
